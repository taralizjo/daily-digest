# Daily Digest — 2026-10-13

## AI

**Amazon in talks to buy Decart for about $7B**: The Wall Street Journal reported on Oct 11 that Amazon is negotiating to acquire Decart, and Reuters relayed the report without independently verifying it. Decart sells software that lets AI models run across Nvidia GPUs, Google TPUs and Amazon's Trainium chips, and builds the Lucy real-time video model and the Oasis world model. PitchBook data cited by the WSJ valued it at nearly $4B in May, and Bloomberg reported that Anthropic looked at buying it for about $6B and declined after due diligence. No deal is signed; a $7B price would signal a premium on chip-agnostic inference software. Source: https://kfgo.com/2026/10/11/amazon-in-talks-to-buy-ai-startup-decart-for-around-7-billion-wsj-reports/

**Nvidia in early talks to buy or deepen its stake in Reflection AI**: The Financial Times reported on Oct 10 that Nvidia is discussing a larger investment in, or an outright purchase of, the open-source model startup, with an acqui-hire (hiring staff and licensing technology) floated as a way to avoid a long antitrust review. Nvidia has put about $800M into Reflection, which launched its first open-weight model, Beam, on Oct 5 and last priced a round at $25B pre-money. Reuters could not verify the report, Bloomberg said terms were not established, and the FT's sources said a deal could come within weeks or fall apart. It would follow Nvidia's roughly $20B Groq licensing deal and its $12.9B Hugging Face purchase. Source: https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/10/11/nvidia-in-talks-to-invest-further-in-reflection-ai-or-buy-it-ft-reports

**UK says it will keep testing OpenAI and Anthropic models before release**: The UK government said on Monday that its AI Security Institute will continue pre-release testing of the most powerful models from OpenAI and Anthropic. The announcement follows US moves to put White House checks first, so Britain is keeping an independent evaluation role rather than deferring to Washington. Details on testing scope and any binding obligations were not available in the coverage reviewed. It matters as one of the few government-run pre-deployment checks on frontier models. Source: https://thenextweb.com/news/uk-ai-security-institute-pre-release-access-openai-anthropic

**Nadella calls for an AI "emergency brake" and independent audits**: Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella posted on X on Oct 10 that authorized people should be able to pause or shut down a model mid-task, backed by independent audits and tamper-proof logs. Microsoft president Brad Smith has made similar arguments, including support for independent pre-launch evaluations and developer liability for catastrophic harm. These are executive statements, not a Microsoft program or a regulatory requirement, and who would run the evaluations is undefined. Source: https://telanganatoday.com/nadella-calls-for-ai-emergency-brake-warns-firms-against-trusting-advanced-models-blindly

## World Affairs

**Houthi missile attack on Riyadh airport kills 12, wounds over 300**: Saudi civil aviation officials said the Oct 10 strike on King Khalid International Airport killed at least 12 people, including four Saudis, two Bangladeshis and one each from the US, Jordan, Palestine, Syria, Sudan and Egypt, and wounded 309. It was the third attack on the airport that week, and an earlier toll of three dead has since been superseded. The Saudi-led coalition called it a "full-fledged war crime" and promised a response; operations at the kingdom's busiest airport were suspended. President Trump said the US would "look at" joining Saudi strikes, but Washington's position is unsettled. Source: https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/10/attack-on-airport-in-saudi-capital-riyadh-leaves-several-injured

**Iran says US reply on reopening the Strait of Hormuz is "insufficient"**: On Oct 11, Iran's Supreme National Security Council secretary Mohsen Rezaei said Washington's answer to Iran's seven conditions fell short, adding that "some of our conditions" were accepted while others remain unclear. The conditions, sent about two weeks ago and relayed by Qatar and Pakistan, have not been officially published; Rezaei has named an end to the war on all fronts, release of frozen Iranian funds and an end to the US naval blockade, and media reports add oil-sanctions relief. Iran says shipping could normalize within seven days once they are met, and the strait stays closed until then. Watch for a further US reply through Qatar. Source: https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/mena/2026/10/11/iran-says-us-response-to-proposal-to-reopen-strait-of-hormuz-is-insufficient/

**Oil and the dollar rise on Gulf escalation ahead of US inflation data**: On Oct 12 the dollar strengthened against the major currencies as oil climbed on the Houthi attacks, with market briefings noting equities slipped on chipmaker weakness; reported oil levels varied by source. Markets are focused on the US CPI release due Wednesday, Oct 14, with the Fed's next meeting on Oct 27-28 and some pricing of a further rate hike. Energy-driven inflation combined with tighter policy pressures bonds and emerging-market currencies; India's rupee fell about 1% and the central bank opened a special forex window for oil companies. Source: https://www.fxcm.com/markets/insights/global-macro-and-markets-briefing-12-october-2026/
